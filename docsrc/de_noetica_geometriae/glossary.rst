Glossarium Technicum (Technical Glossary)
=========================================

.. rubric:: * Lexicon Terminorum Graecorum, Scholasticorum et Mathematicorum *

This technical glossary defines all **99 Greek, Scholastic Latin, German, and French philosophical and mathematical terms** tagged with ``:term:`` across **Father Peter Hoenen, S.J.**'s *De Noetica Geometriae* (1954). Every inline ``:term:`` in the Latin and English editions links directly to its entry below, and each entry provides the original headword, language tradition, English translation, philosophical explanation, and direct back-links (`LA` · `EN`) to every occurrence in the text.

abstractio intuitiva formalis — Intuitive formal abstraction
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Headword**: *abstractio intuitiva formalis* (``abstractio-intuitiva``)
* **Language Tradition**: Latin (Scholastic)
* **English Translation**: *Intuitive formal abstraction*

.. _term-agere-sequitur-esse:

agere sequitur ad esse in actu — Action follows upon actual being
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Headword**: *agere sequitur ad esse in actu* (``agere-sequitur-esse``)
* **Language Tradition**: Latin (Scholastic)
* **English Translation**: *Action follows upon actual being*
* **Noetic & Mathematical Definition**: Classical Thomistic metaphysical axiom affirming that no causal agency or operation can proceed except from an entity that already possesses actual existence (esse in actu).
* **Occurrences in De Noetica Geometriae (4)**: **Appendix**: § 1. (`LA <appendix/appendix.html#occ-term-agere-sequitur-esse-1>`__ · `EN <appendix/appendix-en.html#occ-term-agere-sequitur-esse-1>`__), § 1. .. _term-anairei:

ἀναιρεῖ (ἀναιρεῖν) — Destroys / sublates (hypotheses)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Headword**: *ἀναιρεῖ (ἀναιρεῖν)* (``anairei``)
* **Language Tradition**: Greek
* **English Translation**: *Destroys / sublates (hypotheses)*
* **Noetic & Mathematical Definition**: Plato's term in Republic VII (533c) for the dialectical method, which 'destroys' or transcends the provisional hypotheses of the mathematical sciences in order to ascend to the unhypothetical first principle.
