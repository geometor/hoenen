# Translation Benchmark & Philological Analysis: Praefatio

**Work:** Petrus Hoenen, S.J., *De Noetica Geometriae: Eiusque Cum Rebus Necessitate et Exactitudine* (Rome: Gregorian University Press, 1954)  
**Section:** *Praefatio* (pp. 5–7)  
**Focus:** Philological Commentary, Translation Criteria, and Scholarly Apparatus  

---

## 1. Translation Criteria and Apparatus Standards

The translation of Father Petrus Hoenen's *Praefatio* establishes the technical benchmarks for rendering his Scholastic Latin into English:

1. **Scholastic Metaphysical Precision**: Faithful rendering of Thomistic technical terms (*in actu exercito* / *in actu signato*, *habitus*, *esse actuale*, *obiectum proprium*).
2. **Defect Elimination**: Remediation of line-split artifacts, hyphenation debris, and erroneous footnote tags inherited from physical printings.
3. **English Academic Fluency**: Unrolling periodic, formal Latin clauses into clear academic English without syntactic stiffness or calques.
4. **Scholarly Apparatus Completeness**: Identification, expansion, and translation of bibliographic citations and Latin scholarly conventions (*resp.* = *respectively*, not *or*).

## 3. Philological & Contextual Notes

1. **Preserving Scholastic Technical Vocabulary in Context**:
   - Rather than settling for bare literalisms ("exercised act"), we retain the Latin phrase parenthetically while clarifying the epistemological sense in the English phrasing.
2. **Bibliographic & Historical Context**:
   - *Gregorianum Articles*: Hoenen refers to his earlier series of articles in *Gregorianum* (Vol. 20, 1939; Vol. 34, 1953), which formed the basis of Chapters I–IV and the Appendix.
   - *Hans Freudenthal Debate*: Footnote 1 references the debate between Hoenen and Dutch mathematician Hans Freudenthal in *Gregorianum* (1951), centering on whether intuition can be eliminated from geometric axiomatics.
   - *Citations of Hoenen's Epistemological Works*: Footnote 2 references Hoenen's *Théorie du jugement* (French, 1946; 2nd ed. 1953) and its English translation *Reality and Judgment according to St. Thomas* (1952), cited throughout the treatise as *Th. d. J.* and *R. a J.* respectively.
