# Petrus Hubertus Jacobus Hoenen, S. J.

Peter Hoenen (1880–1961) was a Dutch Jesuit priest (indicated by *S. I.* – *Societas Iesu*, or S.J. in English), philosopher, physicist, and academic.

## Academic Background
Before entering philosophy, Hoenen received rigorous training in theoretical physics, studying under Nobel laureate Hendrik Antoon Lorentz at the University of Leiden, where he defended his doctorate in 1912. This unique background gave him deep technical mastery of the exact sciences, thermodynamics, and mathematical foundations.

## Role
He served for decades as Professor of Philosophy at the Pontifical Gregorian University in Rome (*Pontificia Universitas Gregoriana*), where he held the chair of natural philosophy (cosmology) and epistemology.

## Key Contributions
Hoenen is celebrated for bridging mathematical physics, modern non-Euclidean geometry, and formal axiomatics with the classical realism of Aristotle and St. Thomas Aquinas. His epistemology develops a groundbreaking doctrine of **intuitive formal abstraction** (*abstractio formalis intuitiva*), demonstrating that mathematical first principles and necessity are directly perceived by the intellect within imaginative sensory presentations (*phantasmata* and *materia intelligibilis*).

## Notable Works
* ***Cosmologia*** (Rome: Gregorian University, 1931; 4th ed. 1949; 5th ed. 1956). His standard treatise on the philosophy of nature, treating space (*ubi*), time, continuity, and relativity.
* ***La théorie du jugement d'après St. Thomas d'Aquin*** (Analecta Gregoriana, 1946; 2nd ed. 1953; English trans. *Reality and Judgment According to St. Thomas*, Chicago: Regnery, 1952). His fundamental epistemological work distinguishing the apprehension of quiddity from the affirmation of *esse*.
* ***Recherches de logique formelle*** (Rome, 1947).
* ***Filosofia della natura inorganica*** (Brescia: Morcelliana, 1949).
* ***De origine formae materialis*** (Rome, 1951).
* ***De noetica geometriae: Origine theoriae cognitionis*** (Analecta Gregoriana, Vol. LXIII, Rome, 1954).
* A landmark series of articles in ***Gregorianum*** (1933–1953) on first principles, the Cartesian Cogito, the foundations of geometry, and existential connections.
