## Core Epistemological Breakthroughs

In Chapter 3, Father Petrus Hoenen confronts the second foundational crisis of geometry: **the problem of exactitude** (*problema exactitudinis*). How can the human mind form absolutely exact concepts (unextended points, widthless lines, depthless surfaces, infinite divisibility) when all sensory experience and imaginative phantasms are irremediably inexact?

### 1. The Breakdown of Modern Formalism and Empiricism
* **J. Wellstein and Formalist Voluntarism**: Wellstein argued that because shrinking a grain of sand (*Grenzprozeß*) vanishes into sensory darkness at the *minimum imaginabile*, mathematicians cannot know that a point exists; hence, Euclid's definition of a point (*σημεῖόν ἐστιν οὗ μέρος οὐθέν*) must be **postulated by an arbitrary act of will** (*ein reiner Akt des Willens*).
* **John Stuart Mill and Sensationalism**: Mill argued that because points without magnitude and lines without breadth cannot be perceived or imagined, they are "wholly inconceivable" fictions, reducing our idea of a point to the physical *minimum visibile*.
* **Proclus and Kant**: Proclus rejected Aristotelian abstraction because he thought it failed to account for exactitude, falling back on Platonic recollection; Kant retreated to subjective *a priori* forms.

### 2. The Thomistic Solution: The Deductive Derivation of Indivisibles
Hoenen demonstrates that the intellect does not arrive at points by imagining shrinking particles. It begins with the primary datum: **extended being as extended** (*ens extensum ut extensum*).
1. **Division**: The intellect intuits that extension is divisible. Divide a solid into two parts.
2. **The Boundary (*Limes*)**: Between the parts lies a boundary through which transition occurs.
3. **The Indivisibility of Transition**:
   * Alongside the parts (*iuxta partes*), the boundary is extended.
   * Between the parts (*inter partes*), **it is strictly indivisible**!
   * *Proof*: If the boundary between two parts possessed thickness, the body would be divided not into two parts, but into three parts (the two parts plus the boundary layer), generating an infinite regress.
4. **The Hierarchy of Dimensions**:
   * The boundary of a 3D solid is a **surface** (2D), indivisible in depth.
   * The boundary of a 2D surface is a **line** (1D), indivisible in breadth.
   * The boundary of a 1D line is a **point** (0D), indivisible in all dimensions—an **indivisible having position** (*indivisibile positionem habens*).
5. **Ontological Status**: Indivisibles exist **in potency** in undivided extension, and **in act** upon division. They are neither fictions nor arbitrary postulates; they are real intelligible boundaries.

### 3. The Refutation of the Möbius Strip Objection
Louis Couturat and J. Wellstein objected that a surface cannot be defined as the boundary of a solid, because unilateral surfaces (such as the Möbius strip) have only one side and do not bound any solid.
Hoenen refutes this on two levels:
* **Locally (A. Voss)**: Every local element of a Möbius strip functions as an indivisible boundary separating two regions.
* **Globally / Physically**: Any physical Möbius strip has thickness; it is a **3D solid** bounded by **two distinct Möbius surfaces** (the wide face and the edge-ribbon). When constructed with a thick cross-section, the wide face visibly possesses two sides: one facing outer space, and one facing the interior of the solid! The objection collapses.

### 4. Infinite Divisibility and the Nature of the Continuum
* **A Continuum is Not Composed of Points**: Points have zero extension ($0$). Adding zeroes cannot produce extension ($\sum 0 = 0$). Hence, a line is not an actualized collection of points.
* **Aristotle vs. Cantor**: Rejecting naive set theory (viewing a line as an actual infinity of points), Hoenen aligns with Aristotle, Poincaré, and L. E. J. Brouwer: a line is a **matrix and potentiality** in which points can be actualized.
* **Continuous Motion in Analytical Geometry**: An equation such as $y = \sin x$ represents continuous kinematic motion ($x = t, y = \sin t$), not a static set of points.

### 5. Kinematic Refutation of Poincaré’s Pathological Curves
Henri Poincaré argued that intuition is discredited because analysis discovered continuous curves that lack tangents (such as $y = x \sin(1/x)$ at $x = 0$, and Weierstrass curves).
Hoenen refutes this kinematically:
* To generate $y = x \sin(1/x)$ across the origin, a point would have to execute an **actual infinity of oscillations in finite time**.
* Because an actual infinity cannot be traversed (*infinitum pertransiri nequit*), the curve does not exist geometrically as a completed entity across $x = 0$. Anywhere away from zero, the curve exists and has a tangent. Intuition is completely vindicated.

### 6. Geometry as a Constructive Science
Geometric construction is neither empirical copying nor unconstrained creation *ex nihilo*; it is the **intellective actualization of real potentialities in intelligible matter** (*materia intelligibilis*).

## Chapter 3 Outline

### Part I: On the Existence of Indivisibles
- Distinguishing the problem of exactitude from the problem of necessity.
- Division of the problem: questions independent of the principle of individuation (Ch. 3) vs. questions dependent upon it (Ch. 4).

### § 1. On the Formulation of the Problem
- **1. In What the Problem Consists**: Absolute exactitude of geometric objects vs. inescapable inexactitude of sensory perception and imagination.
- **2. Historical Perspectives**:
  - *Philosophers*: Aristotle’s angle-sum of a triangle (*Post. Anal.* I, 31); Proclus’s rejection of abstraction; Mill’s denial of exactitude and reduction of points to *minimum visibile*.
  - *Mathematicians*: Modern flight to arithmetic analysis; Klein and Poincaré on intuition; Study’s dismissal of classical geometry as a utopia; Wellstein on Euclid’s definition of a point.
- **3. The Solution to be Sought**: The historical presence of exact concepts in the human mind; the question of the mathematical existence of boundaries in extended being.

### § 2. On the Primary Notions of Classical Geometry
- **1. On the Point, Line, and Surface**: Wellstein’s limiting process (*Grenzprozeß*); the claim that points must be postulated by an act of will.
- **2. The Division of Extended Being**:
  - The starting point: 3D extended being as extended (*ens extensum ut extensum*).
  - The boundary (*limes*) between parts: extended *iuxta partes*, strictly indivisible *inter partes*.
  - Deduction of the surface (indivisible in depth), the line (indivisible in breadth), and the point (indivisible in all dimensions).
  - Points as *indivisibilia positionem habentia*; mathematical existence in potency vs. actualization by division.
  - Physical existence of boundaries, lines, and vertices in nature.
- **3. On Dimensions**: Dimensions as the number of successive boundary-divisions an extension sustains; 3D solid, 2D surface, 1D line, 0D point; impossibility of fractional dimensions.
- **4. The Objection Drawn from the Möbius Strip**: Couturat and Wellstein’s objection; local resolution (A. Voss); global resolution (the physical strip as a 3D solid bounded by two Möbius surfaces).
- **5. Consequences for General Epistemology**:
  - Aristotelian abstraction vindicated: intellect reads exact boundaries in the phantasm; sensory cognition as *materia causae* (St. Thomas, *S. Th.* I, q. 84, a. 6).
  - Refutation of Plato, Kant, and Mill.
- **6. On Infinite Divisibility**:
  - Divisibility *ad infinitum* into smaller extensions; why a continuum cannot be divided exhaustively into points.
  - Line as a *locus* (matrix/potentiality) vs. Cantorian collection of points; Brouwer and Poincaré on the actual infinite.
  - Analytical geometry and continuous kinematic motion ($x = t, y = \sin t$).
  - Refutation of Poincaré's objection from non-differentiable curves ($y = x \sin(1/x)$).
- **7. Geometry as a Constructive Science**: Construction as the intellective actualization of real potentialities in intelligible matter.
