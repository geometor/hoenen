1. Translation Criteria and Apparatus Standards¶

The translation of Father Petrus Hoenen’s Praefatio establishes the technical benchmarks for rendering his Scholastic Latin into English:

  1. Scholastic Metaphysical Precision: Faithful rendering of Thomistic technical terms (in actu exercito / in actu signato, habitus, esse actuale, obiectum proprium).

  2. Defect Elimination: Remediation of line-split artifacts, hyphenation debris, and erroneous footnote tags inherited from physical printings.

  3. English Academic Fluency: Unrolling periodic, formal Latin clauses into clear academic English without syntactic stiffness or calques.

  4. Scholarly Apparatus Completeness: Identification, expansion, and translation of bibliographic citations and Latin scholarly conventions (resp. = respectively, not or).

Analytical Note

Opening Definition

Hoc opus agit de « noetica geometriae », i. e. de ea scientiae philosophicae noeticae parte, cuius obiectum est natura cognoscitiva mentis humanae in quantum haec scientiam geometricam construit.

This work deals with the “noetics of geometry,” that is, with that part of philosophical noetic science whose object is the cognitive nature of the human mind insofar as it constructs geometric science.

This work deals with the “noetics of geometry”, that is, that branch of philosophical noetics whose proper object is the cognitive nature of the human mind insofar as it constructs geometric science.

Avoids the repetitive “philosophical noetic science” and specifies “proper object” (obiectum proprium), standard in Scholastic epistemology.

Publishing History

Prima pars eorum, quae exponuntur (cap. I-IV) est editio altera seriei articulorum...

The first part of the things expounded here (chapters I-IV) is a second edition of a series of articles…

The first part of this exposition (Chapters I–IV) represents a second, revised edition of a series of articles…

Replaces awkward literal calque (“The first part of the things expounded here”) with idiomatic scholarly English (“The first part of this exposition”).

Incomplete Systems & Deduction

In his capitibus noetice examinamus axiomata... tantum quoad puncta principalia criticae modernae; haec autem penitus examinantur, sunt enim maioris momenti philosophici in omnibus indagationibus epistemologicis.

In these chapters we noetically examine the axioms… only insofar as they relate to the principal points of modern criticism; these, however, are examined deeply, for they are of greater philosophical importance…

In these chapters, we examine from a noetic standpoint the axioms… focusing strictly on the principal issues raised by modern critique. These, however, are examined thoroughly, for they possess paramount philosophical significance…

Replaces “noetically examine” (an unnatural adverbial construction) with “examine from a noetic standpoint,” and sharpens “criticism” to “critique” (critica).

The Need for Continual Primitive Principles

...menti humanae necessarium esse videtur, semper iterum novas propositiones primitivas, indemonstrabiles et tamen cognitas ut necessarias, invocare ; id non tantum necessarium esse videtur, sed simul, in illis scientiis, possibile ; in infinitum.

…it seems necessary for the human mind always to invoke new primitive propositions again and again, indemonstrable and yet known as necessary; this seems not only necessary, but at the same time, in those sciences, possible; to infinity.

…it proves necessary for the human mind repeatedly to invoke fresh primitive propositions that are indemonstrable yet known with necessity; and in those sciences, this is not only necessary, but continuously possible, ad infinitum.

Replaces redundant stacking “always… again and again” (semper iterum) with tightened cadence: “repeatedly to invoke fresh primitive propositions… known with necessity.”

Scholastic Actus Exercitus vs. Actus Signatus

...adsunt implicite tantum sive « in actu exercito » ; noeticae munus est talia principia explicite statuere, actum exercitum perducere ad « actum signatum ».

…they are present only implicitly, or “in exercised act” (in actu exercito); the task of noetics is to establish such principles explicitly, to lead the exercised act to a “signified act” (actum signatum).

…they are present only implicitly, or “in the exercised act” (in actu exercito); the proper task of noetics is to articulate these principles explicitly, elevating knowledge operative in the exercised act into fully “signified act” (in actum signatum).

Thomistic technical terms. Clarifies the epistemic movement: “elevating knowledge operative in the exercised act into fully signified act.”

Page-Turn Gap Remediation

Cosmologiam scholasticam, sine talibus indagationibus... construi non posse... Sed et pro aliis disciplinis philosophicis hoc exercitium practicum continuum...

Inherited an unstitched gap:

…without such preliminary or simultaneous investigations. But also for other

(blank line)

philosophical disciplines this continuous practical exercise…

Unified into a continuous, coherent paragraph block.

Remedied an OCR/transcription page-break hallucination across pp. 6–7 where discipli- / nis was broken across lines.

Deterrence of Philosophers

Sunt philosophi, forte non pauci, qui ubi in elucubratione philosophica effata geometrica... obviam habent, statim a lectione et studio deterrentur. Ne timeant...

There are philosophers, perhaps not a few, who, when they encounter geometric or generally mathematical pronouncements… are immediately deterred from reading and studying. Let them not fear; this small labor, unless I am mistaken…

There are philosophers (perhaps not a few) who, the moment they encounter geometric formulations or mathematical propositions in a philosophical treatise, even of the simplest kind, are immediately discouraged from reading and study. Let them have no fear: this modest effort will, if I am not mistaken…

Restores academic gravitas to Hoenen’s reassurance to mathematically timid philosophers.

Date & Footnote Remediation

...anno 1953.

…in the year [#f1953]_.

…in the year 1953.

In unedited text, [^1953] became an accidental, broken markdown footnote link. Restored the genuine historical date.

Footnote 1 (Citations)

...responsum ad animadversiones clmi. H. Freudenthal.

…reply to the observations of the very distinguished H. Freudenthal.

…[On the Noetics of Geometry: A Reply to the Remarks of the Most Distinguished H. Freudenthal].

Provides English translations of Latin journal article titles in square brackets for accessibility.

Footnote 2 (Abbreviations)

...Ad id referemus sub siglo Th. d. J. resp. R. a J.

…We will refer to it under the siglum Th. d. J. or R. a J.

…We shall refer to this work under the abbreviations Th. d. J. and R. a J. respectively.

Latin resp. stands for respective (“respectively”), not “or”. The first is the French siglum (Théorie du Jugement), the second is the English (Reality and Judgment).

3. Philological & Contextual Notes¶

3.1. Preserving Scholastic Technical Vocabulary in Context¶

Rather than settling for bare literalisms (“exercised act”), we retain the Latin phrase parenthetically while clarifying the epistemological sense in the English phrasing.

3.2. Bibliographic & Historical Context¶

  • Gregorianum Articles: Hoenen refers to his earlier series of articles in Gregorianum (Vol. 20, 1939; Vol. 34, 1953), which formed the basis of Chapters I–IV and the Appendix.

  • Hans Freudenthal Debate: Footnote 1 references the debate between Hoenen and Dutch mathematician Hans Freudenthal in Gregorianum (1951), centering on whether intuition can be eliminated from geometric axiomatics.

  • Citations of Hoenen’s Epistemological Works: Footnote 2 references Hoenen’s Théorie du jugement (French, 1946; 2nd ed. 1953) and its English translation Reality and Judgment according to St. Thomas (1952), cited throughout the treatise as Th. d. J. and R. a J. respectively.
